## Task 05.01 - Free

### *Summary*

**_The orchid tree_** is a 5-minute silent shortfilm made with procedural generation. It is a visual journey based in the existance of hope in the driest and more challenging moments of our lifes. Not only the human lifes, but the one of the ecosystems of the planet. It is a visualization of how the rough, hard nature can become a threshold to a pilgrimage that takes us to a place where the speed of light and color shows us another perspective from what once was inert.

### *Concept*

**_The orchid tree_** comes from the idea of creating a symbol of hope. The colombian national flower is an orchid and as an epiphyte this plant lives and grows on other plants, in this case on a tree that was dry and is coming back to life. Orchids have a special kind of symbiotic relationship with the trees: they grow on them but doing no harm, they only profit from the structure they offer to live in. They take their nutrients from the air and the sun and are famous colorful and exotic beings.  

A sick ecosystem can survive with care, represented in social, political, cultural and even artistic human action. I created this world to immortalize the impact of our decisions and as an invitation to think and feel not only focused on us, but also on the beings around us. In the most dificult times it is helpful to remember that we live in a dynamic world and everything is in motion... all the time. This is a journey to remember that we can always transform the way we look at things. 

### *Implementation*
I explored the procedural generation for the creation of landscapes. My tools were Unreal engine, Davinci Resolve and youtube, where I found the tutorials. 

I used Unreal engine as my tool to take vegetation assets and use them as the base for the spawning possibilities of the engine. I worked with 2 different but similar kinds of procedural generation tools following some tutorials, but of course using the new knowledge in a personalized way to create my own approach. The first one is the work with cloners and effectors to place the different types of vegetation on the meshes of the dry trunks and tree to later clone them with some variations in order to give the whole a more natural look. The effectors also help to define the limits of the cloners, so they are in the parts of the mesh that I wanted them to be. 


Here the tutorials:

The second procedural generation tool is related with the Procedural Foliage Spawner, which allowed me to spawn the vegetation of my environment. I used noise-based tools of Unreal to deform my landscape and create a height field to make it more organic. Then I created a procedural foliage volume with my own selection of vegetation assets to create the atmosphere.

### *Project Reflection & Discussion*


The **_tree of orchids_** is a project that allowed me to challenge myself by creating something based on my previous knowledge in film, television, camera and storytelling with procedural generation processes.  All the topics we learned in this lecture are or at least can be very easily put together in order to create digital art, everything is pretty much connected. For instance, the variations created by the spawner, the cloners and the effectors are related to functions, mathematics and noise. I even was thinking about the way I can create patterns which are perceived as natural: both chaotic and organized. I also wanted to integrate particles in the system, like insects around the ecosystem, but it was very challenging for me to understand how to optimize in the creation and render in Unreal, so that is one idea for the future. 

I struggled a lot trying to solve performance issues and my lack of knowledge and the ocean of tutorials and information were sometimes more overwhelming than helpful. However, I was able to make it work and I am happy with the result. 

One of the things that made the difference was to render the project in the same hard drive where Unreal is installed on. I would like to learn more about the ways in which we can optimize these processes, since I felt limited due to the performance of the computer.

I would like to learn more about creating with mathematics and functions in the shaders for example. Also, it would be nice to know how I could affect both kinds of procedural generation processes at the time, since I was not able to do it and I was forced to drop some ideas like to show the growing of the healthy environment across the whole ecosystem at the same time, but I could not find enough information about it, so I showed the dry world followed by the healthy one in two different moments. I am very proud of the result, but it would be nice to know, because if I would also be working with shaders it would be even more complex. I guess it has to do with blueprints, but I am not still ready for that step, since I am still learning. 

I would like to mention my desire in working with physics and different forces and the ways in which they affect the landscape: wind, gravity, rain, etc. some assets in Unreal have already an integrated movement which is amazing, but it would also nice to know how that works and to make it part of the project without making the software crash. 

Finally I would like to mention that this project was part of a very challenging experience of mine which ended good, so I wanted to transmit the journey from the rigid and inert to the motion and liveliness.  


### *Lessons Learned*

I learned how to take an asset and use it as the seed for the creation of a natural pattern in which the variation was achieved with the intrinsic noise and function settings of Unreal's spawning tools like rate, rotation, scale and position. 

I was able to put in practice different ways of creating with  procedural generation and I found a way of connecting my previous knowledge with the new tools, which I think is very satisfying and made me feel good about the process.

Additionaly, I was able to explore the poetic of the digital art, which I think is something meaningful and important to me.
